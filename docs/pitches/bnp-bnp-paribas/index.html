01

Overview

BNP Paribas entered the portfolio at the December 2025 rebalance. Banks are scored only on book-to-price and earnings-to-price in our process, because enterprise value and free cash flow do not make sense for a bank. BNP ranked 5th of 203 financials. This note explains how a universal bank earns money, why its share price moves with French bonds, and how to value it from its tangible book.

BNP earned a 13.2% return on tangible equity over the first nine months of 2026, ahead of its own 12% target, yet trades below tangible book because the market treats it as a proxy for French sovereign risk. With CET1 at 12.9% and a €1.3bn buyback starting, we see value in the gap.

02

The business

BNP Paribas is the largest bank in the euro area. It has three divisions. Commercial, Personal Banking & Services (CPBS) collects deposits and lends to households and companies in four home markets (France, Belgium, Italy and Luxembourg) and runs specialised businesses such as consumer credit and Arval, a car-leasing company.

Corporate & Institutional Banking (CIB) advises and finances large companies, trades bonds, currencies and equities for clients (Global Markets), and holds assets for institutions (Securities Services). It is about 38% of revenue and the most volatile part of earnings.

Investment & Protection Services (IPS) sells insurance, manages wealth and runs asset management, now including AXA IM. These are fee businesses that need little capital, which is why management keeps adding to them.

Commercial, Personal Banking & Services

Retail networks in France, Belgium, Italy and Luxembourg, Europe-Mediterranean, plus Personal Finance, Arval (car leasing, Athlon acquired July 2026) and Nickel.

Corporate & Institutional Banking

Global Banking, Global Markets and Securities Services; the bank describes itself as the leading EMEA investment bank.

Investment & Protection Services

Insurance, Wealth Management and Asset Management, including AXA IM, consolidated since July 2025.

Capital

CET1 12.9% at September 2026, against a 10.45% requirement; guided above 13% at end-2026.

03

Industry & macro

French government bonds sold off in 2026 on political and budget uncertainty. The 10-year yield reached about 4.9% and the spread over German Bunds about 155 bp. Investors sell French banks when the sovereign weakens, because banks hold government bonds, fund themselves at a spread to the sovereign, and depend on the domestic economy.

For BNP the direct exposure is limited: about €19bn of French government bonds, €16bn of them at amortised cost so price moves do not hit capital. The indirect effect is through funding costs and the risk premium investors demand.

The Middle East conflict pushed Brent to about $115 a barrel. That raises inflation risk and could slow the ECB's path, which helps net interest income but hurts credit quality. BNP's cost of risk was 39 bp in Q3 2026, at the top of its guidance.

06

Investment thesis

Returns are ahead of target

9M 2026 RoTE of 13.2% and a 59.0% cost/income ratio beat the 2026 targets (~12% and ~60%). In February 2026 the bank raised its 2028 targets to RoTE above 13% and cost/income below 56%.

The price is a sovereign discount, not a bank discount

Shares fell about 20% in the three months to late October 2026, to a 28-week low of €85.35, with no company-specific cause found, while French 10-year yields reached ~4.9% and the OAT-Bund spread ~155 bp. French government bonds held total about €19bn, €16bn of them at amortised cost.

Capital comes back to shareholders

2026 payout of 60% (at least 50% dividend, 10% buyback), a €1.3bn buyback from November 2026, and from 2027 the distribution of CET1 above 13%. A new 2030 plan is due on 2 February 2027.

Variant perception

Where our reading differs from the obvious one.

What the market says
What the evidence says
“BNP is a leveraged bet on France.”
Three of its four retail home markets are outside France, CIB is the leading EMEA investment bank, and IPS adds fee income. Earnings are more diversified than the share price implies.
“Trading revenue is at a peak.”
Global Markets is strong (+21.9% in Q3), but the RoTE target was beaten even after €408m of restructuring costs.
“The Sudan litigation is an open-ended liability.”
It is real and unprovisioned; we treat it as a tail risk that the appeal (2027) will clarify, and keep the position sized accordingly.

Valuation

Method: Justified price to tangible book (Gordon growth). A bank is worth (RoTE − g) / (cost of equity − g) times its tangible book. We use the third-party tangible book per share of €98.7 at June 2026 (third-party estimate: the bank's release does not state it), long-term growth of 2%, and a cost of equity that falls as the French risk premium normalises.

The bear case, taken seriously

The bear case: France is in a slow sovereign squeeze, BNP's earnings include one-offs (an €858m gain on Ageas, a €372m Allfunds revaluation) and peak trading, and the Sudan verdict could become a class-wide liability. In that world a bank at 0.9x tangible book is fairly priced, not cheap.

12

Interview prep

Questions a portfolio manager is likely to ask, with short answers.

Why do you value BNP on price to tangible book and not P/E?
Because a bank's earning power is its equity times its return. Justified P/TBV = (RoTE − g) / (CoE − g) links the two: a 12% RoTE with an 11.5% cost of equity justifies about 1.05x.
What drives the discount?
French sovereign risk. The share price fell 20% in a quarter without bad company news, while French yields rose.
What would make you wrong?
RoTE falling below 12%, CET1 below 12.5%, or a material Sudan provision.
Why did your model give BNP 4% in mean-variance and 0.5% in minimum variance?
Mean-variance rewards the high expected return from the score; minimum variance penalises the bank's high correlation with the rest of the European book.

Glossary

RoTE
Return on tangible equity: net income divided by equity excluding goodwill and intangibles.
CET1 ratio
Core equity capital divided by risk-weighted assets; the regulator's main solvency measure.
Cost of risk
Loan-loss provisions as a share of outstanding loans, in basis points.
P/TBV
Price divided by tangible book value per share; the standard bank valuation multiple.
