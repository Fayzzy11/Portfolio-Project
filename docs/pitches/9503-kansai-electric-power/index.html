Kansai Electric Power

Japan's nuclear leader is modestly cheap, once record 2025 profits are normalised

In one paragraph

Kansai runs 45% of Japan's operating nuclear capacity and trades at 0.85x book. But FY2025 was a peak year (84% nuclear capacity factor, fuel time-lag gains), and the company's own plan guides ordinary profit down 44% in FY2026 and ROE to about 8-9%. On normalised returns the upside is single-digit: we keep the model weight.

Investment thesis

A unique asset

Seven pressurised-water reactors (6,578 MW), 45% of Japan's operating nuclear capacity. Each point of capacity factor is worth about ¥5.7bn of ordinary profit.

But the trailing earnings are a peak

FY2025 ordinary profit of ¥518.5bn benefited from an 84% capacity factor and fuel time-lag gains. The FY2026 guide is ¥290bn and the 2026-28 plan averages ¥270bn. Trailing P/E overstates value.

Capital returns are steady, not large

A 25-35% payout, a progressive dividend (¥80 guided) and at least ¥270bn of returns over FY2026-28, funded partly by asset sales. Yield 2.9%.

Valuation

Justified price to book (Gordon growth). A regulated-plus-merchant utility is worth (ROE − g) / (cost of equity − g) times book. We take book per share implied by the 0.85x third-party P/B, long-term growth of 1%, a 6.5-7% cost of equity, and ROE between the normalised level (~5.5-6.5%) and the plan's ~8.5%.

Catalysts and risks

  • 1 Nov 2026

    Wheeling tariff uplift

  • Late Jan 2027

    Q3 FY2026: does the fuel time lag reverse as crude eases?

  • End Apr 2027

    FY2026 results, FY2027 guide and first full year under the 25-35% payout

  • FY2026-28

    Further shareholding sales (¥230bn of a ¥380bn+ target done)

  • Ongoing

    Reactor restart dates driving the capacity factor back toward 70%+

We change the call if
  • Upgrade if ROE guidance moves back above 9% with capacity factor above 75%
  • Downgrade on a new equity raise or a nuclear injunction
  • Downgrade if the progressive dividend policy is abandoned
