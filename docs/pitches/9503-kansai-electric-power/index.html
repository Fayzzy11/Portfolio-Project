01

Overview

Kansai Electric entered the portfolio in June 2025 and ranked first of 59 utilities in December 2025. Unlike the case studies, it is a solid business; the question is how much of its cheapness survives once you normalise a record year. This note explains how a Japanese utility earns money, why nuclear availability dominates the numbers, and why our call is Neutral.

Kansai runs 45% of Japan's operating nuclear capacity and trades at 0.85x book. But FY2025 was a peak year (84% nuclear capacity factor, fuel time-lag gains), and the company's own plan guides ordinary profit down 44% in FY2026 and ROE to about 8-9%. On normalised returns the upside is single-digit: we keep the model weight.

02

The business

Kansai Electric supplies the Osaka-Kyoto-Kobe region. It generates power (nuclear, thermal and hydro), sells it to households and businesses, and owns the regulated transmission and distribution network through Kansai T&D.

Its edge is nuclear: seven reactors at Takahama, Ohi and Mihama, 45% of Japan's operating nuclear capacity. Nuclear power is cheap once plants run, so every point of capacity factor (the share of the year the reactors produce) adds about ¥5.7bn of profit. When reactors are in outage, the company burns more expensive fuel.

Japanese utilities report 'ordinary profit', which includes financial income and equity-method results. Fuel costs are passed to customers with a 3-5 month lag, which creates 'time-lag' gains when fuel prices fall and losses when they rise.

Energy

Generation, retail and gas: ¥377bn of the ¥519bn FY2025 ordinary profit. Generation mix 49% nuclear, 37% thermal, 14% hydro.

Transmission & distribution

Regulated network (Kansai T&D): ¥63bn. Revenue cap raised by ¥149.6bn from November 2026.

ICT

Optage fibre and data centres: ¥47bn.

Real estate

Kanden Realty: ¥36bn; more than ¥550bn of property to be recycled under the 2026 plan.

03

Industry & macro

Japan restarted nuclear plants slowly after Fukushima (2011). Kansai is the most advanced, but older units (Takahama 1 and 2 are over 50 years old) need regulatory approvals and are exposed to court injunctions.

The 2026 Middle East conflict pushed crude to an average of $108 a barrel in H1 FY2026, against an $80 plan assumption, and the yen weakened to ¥160. Combined with outages, ordinary profit fell 65% in H1.

Data centres and electrification are the long-term demand story, but they have not yet moved total Kansai-area demand, which is flat at about 133 TWh.

06

Investment thesis

A unique asset

Seven pressurised-water reactors (6,578 MW), 45% of Japan's operating nuclear capacity. Each point of capacity factor is worth about ¥5.7bn of ordinary profit.

But the trailing earnings are a peak

FY2025 ordinary profit of ¥518.5bn benefited from an 84% capacity factor and fuel time-lag gains. The FY2026 guide is ¥290bn and the 2026-28 plan averages ¥270bn. Trailing P/E overstates value.

Capital returns are steady, not large

A 25-35% payout, a progressive dividend (¥80 guided) and at least ¥270bn of returns over FY2026-28, funded partly by asset sales. Yield 2.9%.

Variant perception

Where our reading differs from the obvious one.

What the market says
What the evidence says
“Data centres will drive a demand boom.”
Kansai-area demand is flat: 133.7 TWh in FY2025 and 133.1 TWh forecast for FY2026.
“The H1 collapse is temporary.”
Partly: the −¥87bn fuel time lag should reverse. But outages (62.7% capacity factor) and ¥2.5tn of capex are structural.
“At 0.85x book it must be cheap.”
Only if ROE holds near 9%; the plan itself guides ROE down to about 8.7%, and normalised ROE is closer to 6-7%.

Valuation

Method: Justified price to book (Gordon growth). A regulated-plus-merchant utility is worth (ROE − g) / (cost of equity − g) times book. We take book per share implied by the 0.85x third-party P/B, long-term growth of 1%, a 6.5-7% cost of equity, and ROE between the normalised level (~5.5-6.5%) and the plan's ~8.5%.

The bear case, taken seriously

The bear case: FY2025 was the peak, ordinary profit averages about half of it over the plan, capex keeps FCF negative and could force another equity raise, and the stock depends on ageing reactors and court decisions.

12

Interview prep

Questions a portfolio manager is likely to ask, with short answers.

Why is Kansai Neutral when it screens as the cheapest utility?
The screen used peak FY2024-25 earnings. On normalised ROE of about 6.5% the justified P/B is about 0.92x, close to today's 0.85x.
What is the most important number for Kansai?
The nuclear capacity factor: each point is worth about ¥5.7bn of ordinary profit.
Why did the minimum-variance portfolio give it 4%?
Utilities have low volatility and low correlation with cyclicals, so a risk-minimising optimiser loads them to the cap.
What would make you upgrade?
Capacity factor back above 75% and ROE guidance above 9%, with no new equity raise.

Glossary

Capacity factor
Actual output divided by the maximum possible output over a period.
Ordinary profit
Japanese profit line: operating profit plus financial income and equity-method results.
Fuel time lag
Delay between fuel-cost changes and the tariff adjustment passed to customers.
Justified P/B
(ROE − g) / (cost of equity − g): the price-to-book a company deserves for its returns.
