01

Overview

Renault is the clearest example in our portfolio of a stock that is cheap because the market does not trust the balance sheet to stay in the hands of shareholders. Our systematic score ranked it first of 102 Consumer Discretionary stocks in December 2025, and the stock has been in the top quintile of its sector at every rebalance since 2021. This note explains how the company makes money, why the market is sceptical, and what has to happen for the discount to close.

Renault trades at about €7.7bn while its car business holds €6.6bn of net cash and its captive bank earns roughly €1.5bn of operating profit a year. The market prices the 2026 margin trough as permanent; we think the reset is done and a product cycle is starting.

02

The business

Renault Group sells about 2.3 million vehicles a year (2.337m in 2025) under three brands. Renault is the core volume brand, Dacia is the low-cost brand built around simple platforms and low equipment levels, and Alpine is a small sports-car brand. Most sales are in Europe; Renault has no US business.

The group has two reporting segments since 2026. Automotive makes and sells cars. Mobilize Financial Services (MFS, the former RCI Banque) lends to customers and dealers to finance those cars. MFS is a bank: it takes deposits, funds itself in the bond market and earns a spread. This is why enterprise-value multiples on Renault mislead: you cannot add a bank's debt to a carmaker's equity value and call it an enterprise value.

The third asset is a stake of roughly 35% in Nissan, according to press reports. After the 2023-2025 Alliance restructuring, the stake is held at fair value through equity. Renault took a €9.3bn non-cash accounting loss on it in 2025, which is why reported net income was about −€10.9bn even though the business made an operating margin of 6.3%.

Automotive (~88% of revenue)

Renault, Dacia and Alpine brands; a volume player in European B and C segments, with Dacia as the low-cost brand. 52% of European sales were electrified in H1 2026.

Mobilize Financial Services (~12%)

Captive car finance and leasing. Contributed €753m of operating margin in H1 2026 and pays dividends up to the car business (€250m in H1 2026, about €500m a year targeted).

Nissan stake

About 35% after the 2023-2025 Alliance restructuring, according to press reports. Carried at fair value through equity since June 2025.

Geography

No US sales, so US tariffs are a second-order risk. Growth markets are India, Turkey, Morocco and Brazil (Geely partnership in Brazil since September 2026).

03

Industry & macro

European car demand is flat. The pressure comes from Chinese manufacturers such as BYD and Geely, which sell electric cars at prices European brands struggle to match. Dacia is the most exposed because it competes on price: its volumes fell 12.8% in Q3 2026 while the group fell 6.0%.

Costs moved against carmakers in 2026. The Middle East conflict lifted raw materials, energy and freight, and Renault guided to a second half only slightly better than the first. The EU's CO2 rules push the mix toward electric cars, which are less profitable today; Renault's electric share reached 25.8% of passenger cars in Q3 2026.

Rates matter through MFS. Its margin depends on the spread between lending rates and its funding cost, and on used-car prices for leased vehicles. The S&P upgrade to BBB− in December 2025 lowers that funding cost.

Why the screen picked it

At the 31 December 2025 rebalance our lag rule (fiscal year end + 6 months) meant the score used FY2024 accounts. Renault scored above +3 standard deviations on EV/EBIT, book-to-price, FCF yield and sales-to-EV versus its sector. The EV/EBIT of 1.8x is not a real number: the enterprise value nets the automotive cash but ignores the bank's structure. Read the score as a flag, then rebuild the value by parts.

06

Investment thesis

The balance sheet is the floor

Automotive net cash of €6.6bn at June 2026 equals about 86% of the market value. Mobilize Financial Services (the former RCI Banque) adds about €1.5bn of annual operating margin and pays dividends to the car business.

The reset is done

Guidance fell from at least 7% margin (early 2025) to about 5.5% for 2026. That guide was confirmed in February, July and October 2026, and the futuREady plan sets a 5-7% through-cycle band. Expectations are now low and credible.

A product cycle is starting

futuREady plans 36 launches by 2030 on a two-year development cycle. Clio VI, Boreal, Twingo E-Tech and Duster are already on sale, and Q3 2026 revenue beat consensus (+1.4% at constant FX versus −0.3% expected).

Variant perception

Where our reading differs from the obvious one.

What the market says
What the evidence says
“Chinese competition will crush margins.”
Volumes are under pressure, but pricing and mix held. The plan cuts variable cost by about €400 per car a year and development time to two years.
“The cash is trapped and will be burnt.”
The car business generated €1.5bn of free cash flow in 2025, S&P upgraded Renault to investment grade (BBB−) in December 2025, and the €2.20 dividend was paid from that cash.
“Management keeps cutting guidance.”
The new CEO reset targets once, in July 2025. The 2026 guide has since been confirmed three times.
“Nissan is a black hole.”
The stake was written down by €9.3bn in 2025. Our valuation gives it no value, so any monetisation is upside.

Valuation

Method: P/E on 2027 earnings, cross-checked with a sum of the parts. We value Renault on 2027 earnings per share at a single-digit P/E, the range European volume carmakers have traded in through the cycle. Consensus implies about €6.6 of 2026 EPS (price / 4.3x); we use €6.0 in the base case to stay below the street. The sum of the parts is a sanity check, not the target.

The bear case, taken seriously

The bear case is a value trap: margins trend down (7.6% in 2024, 6.3% in 2025, ~5.5% in 2026), the low-cost niche is under attack from China, and the net cash is spent on product launches instead of returned. The medium-term band starts at 5%, so a bad year would sit inside management's own range.

12

Interview prep

Questions a portfolio manager is likely to ask, with short answers.

Why is Renault's EV/EBIT of 1.8x misleading?
Because the group consolidates a bank. Its debt is the bank's funding, not leverage of the car business, so a single EV is meaningless. We value net cash, MFS and the car business separately.
What is the single number that would make you sell?
A 2026 operating margin below 5%, the bottom of management's own band. It would mean the reset was not enough.
Why not value the Nissan stake?
Its value is volatile and its monetisation has no timetable. Giving it zero keeps the thesis independent of Nissan.
How does a value screen end up holding a carmaker for five years?
Carmakers trade on low multiples through the cycle. Renault was in the top quintile of its sector at every date, so the real question is whether the discount closes, which needs cash returned to shareholders.

Glossary

Operating margin
Renault's main profit measure: operating profit before restructuring and one-offs, divided by revenue.
Automotive free cash flow
Cash generated by the car business after capex and R&D, including the dividend received from MFS.
Captive finance arm
A bank owned by a manufacturer to finance its customers and dealers (here Mobilize Financial Services).
Sum of the parts
Valuing each business separately and adding them, instead of applying one multiple to the whole group.
