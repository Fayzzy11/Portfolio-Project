01

Overview

HP has been in the portfolio at all ten rebalances since June 2021: it is a permanent resident of the cheap end of the technology sector. In December 2025 it ranked 2nd of 128 IT stocks. This note explains the two halves of the business, why the market prices them for decline, and why the free-cash-flow yield is the right way to value it.

HP generates about $3bn of free cash flow a year and returns most of it. At $32 the stock trades at about 10x fiscal 2026 earnings and a 10.6% FCF yield, as if printing were about to collapse. We think memory-cost pressure peaks now and the cost programme supports margins into fiscal 2027.

02

The business

HP Inc is the PC and printer half of the old Hewlett-Packard, split in 2015. Personal Systems sells notebooks, desktops and workstations to companies and consumers. It is large ($38.5bn) but earns thin margins (about 5%), because PCs are assembled from components bought from others, mostly processors and memory.

Printing sells printers and, more importantly, the ink and toner they consume. Supplies are a recurring revenue stream with high margins (the segment earns 18-19%). Page volumes are falling as offices print less, so the segment shrinks about 2% a year.

HP returns most of its cash: about two thirds of free cash flow through dividends and buybacks. Years of buybacks have pushed book equity below zero, which is why P/B is meaningless and why our model scored HP on the other three ratios.

Personal Systems

PCs, workstations and peripherals: $38.5bn of FY2025 revenue at a 5.3% operating margin.

Printing

Supplies (ink and toner), hardware and industrial graphics: $16.7bn at an 18.7% margin.

Growth areas

AI PCs with dedicated neural processors, workstations and services.

Capital

Negative book equity after years of buybacks; net debt about $5bn (own calculation).

03

Industry & macro

Microsoft ended Windows 10 support in October 2025, which forced companies to replace old PCs. Personal Systems revenue rose 8%, 11% and then 18% in successive quarters, partly from higher prices.

The headwind is memory. DRAM and NAND prices rose sharply in 2026, and PC makers pay more for every machine. HP expects the peak in Q4 FY2026 (August to October).

Tariffs were the 2025 shock: HP moved production out of China. In February 2026 the Supreme Court ruled that IEEPA does not authorise tariffs, and HP is receiving refunds, which flatter FY2026 EPS by $0.19 one-off.

06

Investment thesis

Cash yield is the valuation

FY2026 FCF guidance was raised to $3.0-3.2bn in August 2026: about 10.6% of the market cap at the midpoint. HP returned $1.9bn (66% of FCF) to shareholders in FY2025 and raised the dividend to $0.30 a quarter.

The margin squeeze is temporary

Management expects DRAM and NAND costs to peak in Q4 FY2026 (Aug-Oct) and Personal Systems margins (4.6% in Q3) to recover sequentially in FY2027. The $1bn gross savings programme runs to FY2028.

Printing still funds the story

Printing is 30% of revenue but earns an 18-19% margin. It declines about 2% a year, slowly enough to keep funding buybacks and dividends.

07

Variant perception

Where our reading differs from the obvious one.

What the market says
What the evidence says
“Printing is in terminal decline.”
Revenue falls about 2% a year and the margin holds above 18%. A slow decline is priced as a fast one.
“FY2026 PC demand was pulled forward by Windows 10.”
Partly true. We use a base FCF of $3.0bn, below the guided midpoint, and exclude tariff refunds.
“An interim CEO means strategic risk.”
A permanent appointment, expected in the coming months, is more likely a catalyst than a threat.

Valuation

Method: Free-cash-flow yield. HP's value to an owner is the cash it can distribute. We divide sustainable free cash flow by a required yield (7.5-10%) and by 901.8m shares. The base case uses $3.0bn, the bottom of the guide, and excludes tariff refunds.

  • Printing margin below 16%
  • A dividend or buyback cut, or FY2027 EPS guide down y/y ex tariff refunds

The bear case, taken seriously

The bear case is that FY2026 was the best year of the PC cycle, helped by Windows 10 and price increases, while printing keeps shrinking. Strip out the tariff refunds and FY2026 EPS is close to the original guide. With negative equity and an interim CEO, there is little room for error.

12

Interview prep

Questions a portfolio manager is likely to ask, with short answers.

Why value HP on FCF yield rather than P/E?
Because the equity story is cash returned to owners, and FCF is less distorted by restructuring charges and tariff refunds.
Isn't printing a melting ice cube?
Yes, but slowly: about −2% a year with an 18% margin. At a 10% FCF yield the market prices a much faster melt.
What will you watch at the November results?
The FY2027 FCF guide (sell trigger below $2.5bn) and the memory-cost commentary.
Why did the model ignore negative book equity?
Book-to-price was negative and counted against HP (z −1.2), but the other three ratios dominated the composite.

Glossary

Free-cash-flow yield
Free cash flow divided by market cap; the cash return an owner would earn at today's price.
Non-GAAP EPS
Earnings per share excluding restructuring, amortisation and other items management treats as non-recurring.
Supplies
Ink and toner cartridges: the recurring, high-margin part of printing.
Negative equity
Liabilities exceed assets on the balance sheet, here because buybacks exceeded retained earnings.
