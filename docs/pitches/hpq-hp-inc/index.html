In one paragraph

HP generates about $3bn of free cash flow a year and returns most of it. At $32 the stock trades at about 10x fiscal 2026 earnings and a 10.6% FCF yield, as if printing were about to collapse. We think memory-cost pressure peaks now and the cost programme supports margins into fiscal 2027.

Investment thesis

Cash yield is the valuation

FY2026 FCF guidance was raised to $3.0-3.2bn in August 2026: about 10.6% of the market cap at the midpoint. HP returned $1.9bn (66% of FCF) to shareholders in FY2025 and raised the dividend to $0.30 a quarter.

The margin squeeze is temporary

Management expects DRAM and NAND costs to peak in Q4 FY2026 (Aug-Oct) and Personal Systems margins (4.6% in Q3) to recover sequentially in FY2027. The $1bn gross savings programme runs to FY2028.

Printing still funds the story

Printing is 30% of revenue but earns an 18-19% margin. It declines about 2% a year, slowly enough to keep funding buybacks and dividends.

Valuation

Free-cash-flow yield. HP's value to an owner is the cash it can distribute. We divide sustainable free cash flow by a required yield (7.5-10%) and by 901.8m shares. The base case uses $3.0bn, the bottom of the guide, and excludes tariff refunds.

Catalysts and risks

  • ~25 Nov 2026

    Q4 FY2026 results and first FY2027 outlook

  • Q4 FY26-H1 FY27

    Memory-cost peak and sequential PC margin recovery

  • 2026-2027

    Appointment of a permanent CEO

  • To FY2028

    $1bn gross run-rate savings, reported each quarter

  • FY2027

    AI PC mix and the tail of the commercial refresh

We change the call if
  • FY2027 FCF guide below about $2.5bn
  • Printing margin below 16%
  • A dividend or buyback cut, or FY2027 EPS guide down y/y ex tariff refunds
