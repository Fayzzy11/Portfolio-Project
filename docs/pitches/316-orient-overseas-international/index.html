In one paragraph

OOIL (OOCL) had the top score of our entire universe in December 2025. The screen saw a P/E under 4x on FY2024 profit; FY2025 profit then fell 41% and H1 2026 another 24%. A 2026 war shock lifted freight rates again, but on normalised earnings the stock is not cheap. We hold the model weight floor and expect mean reversion.

Investment thesis

Why the model bought it

With a six-month lag, the December 2025 score used FY2024 accounts: EBIT of US$2.6bn. FY2025 then delivered US$1.5bn. On the earnings actually being produced the stock was about 40% less cheap than the screen showed.

Earnings are a freight rate times a volume

Profit rose more than 60-fold from the 2019 operating base to the 2022 peak, then fell 86% in a year. A screen that buys the lowest trailing P/E in shipping buys just after peak earnings.

2026 was rescued by a war, not by value

The US-Iran war and Hormuz disruption pushed Asia-US rates up 109% by June 2026 and Q3 liner revenue up 45%. Fleet growth (4.6%) still runs ahead of demand (4.1%), and ships are returning to Suez.

Valuation

P/E on normalised earnings. For a liner, value the average year, not the last one. We average profit over 2019-2025 excluding the 2021-22 supercycle and the 2019 one-off gain (about US$1.3bn, or US$1.99 per share), then apply 7-9x and convert at HK$7.8 per US$.
