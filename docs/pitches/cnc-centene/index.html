01

Overview

Centene is in this pitch set as a case study. It shows the main weakness of a value screen that uses lagged accounts: when earnings break fast, trailing multiples fall faster than the price only because the earnings are stale. Centene ranked first of 108 health-care stocks in December 2025 and was held at all ten rebalances. This note explains how a managed-care insurer makes money, what went wrong in 2025 and what the episode teaches.

In December 2025 Centene was the cheapest health-care stock in our universe, on FY2024 earnings that no longer existed. 2025 adjusted EPS collapsed to $2.08 before a 2026 repricing rebound to a guided floor above $5.20. The stock has recovered; at $67.60 it is fairly priced, and the lesson is about process.

02

The business

Centene is a health insurer paid by governments and individuals to cover medical costs. It receives a premium per member and pays the doctors, hospitals and pharmacies. The key number is the health benefits ratio (HBR): medical costs divided by premiums. At 90% the insurer keeps 10 cents per dollar to cover admin costs and profit; at 93% there is almost nothing left.

Medicaid (health cover for low-income Americans, run by states) is the core: 11.9m members. The Marketplace (Ambetter) sells subsidised individual plans under the Affordable Care Act. Medicare covers people over 65, mostly through stand-alone drug plans (Part D) at Centene.

Two technical mechanisms drive surprises. Risk adjustment moves money between insurers depending on how sick their members are, and the data arrives months after the year. State Medicaid rates are reset with a lag, so when costs jump the insurer absorbs them for a while.

03

Industry & macro

US health policy moved against Centene in 2025-2026. The enhanced ACA premium tax credits expired at the end of 2025, and Marketplace membership fell from 5.5m to 3.4m. Healthier members tend to leave first, which worsens the risk pool.

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 2025) brings Medicaid work requirements and more frequent eligibility checks from end-2026. Fewer members means less revenue, and the members who remain are usually sicker.

Medical cost trends rose across the industry: behavioural health, home health and expensive drugs. On the positive side, the final 2027 Medicare Advantage rate notice (+2.48%) was much better than the January proposal.

05

Why the screen picked it

On the December 2025 score Centene had z-scores of +4.1 on book-to-price, +4.0 on earnings-to-price, +4.4 on sales-to-EV and +3.2 on EBIT-to-EV, but −1.8 on FCF yield. The lag rule meant FY2024 accounts were used, before the 2025 collapse. EV/Sales is structurally low for any insurer (about $195bn of revenue on a $33bn market cap) and says nothing about margins. A revision or quality filter (estimate momentum, HBR trend, goodwill-to-equity) would have flagged the break.

06

Investment thesis

Why the model bought it

With a six-month accounting lag, the December 2025 score used FY2024 accounts: pre-collapse EBIT and book equity before a $6.7bn goodwill impairment. EV/EBIT, P/B and E/P all screened at the top of the sector.

What actually happened

In July 2025 Centene withdrew guidance on Marketplace risk adjustment and Medicaid cost trends. 2025 adjusted EPS came in at $2.08. Repricing then worked: the 2026 floor was raised four times, from $3.00 to $5.20.

Where it stands now

At $67.60 the stock trades at 13x a 2026 figure that includes about $0.50 of non-recurring items. Membership is shrinking (−8% y/y) and Medicaid still runs at a 93.6% benefits ratio. Upside and downside look balanced.

07

Variant perception

Where our reading differs from the obvious one.

What the market says
What the evidence says
“The stock has doubled, so the model was right.”
The 2026 rebound came from repricing and policy, not from the cheapness the model measured. The signal had no defence against a fast fundamental break.
“Managed care is a stable compounder.”
Margins are thin: each 10 bp of benefits ratio on ~$175bn of premiums is about $175m of pre-tax profit.
“Guidance raises mean upside.”
The floor format ('greater than $5.20') and ~$0.50 of non-recurring items flatter 2026; FY2027 guidance in February is the real test.

The bear case, taken seriously

Even after the recovery, the bear case is credible: membership is shrinking, Medicaid margins are negative on an HBR basis, 2026 includes one-offs, and only $175m of cash sits outside regulated subsidiaries.

12

Interview prep

Questions a portfolio manager is likely to ask, with short answers.

Your model held Centene through a 2025 collapse. What does that tell you?
That lagged trailing multiples cannot see a fast fundamental break. The fix is to add a quality or revision signal, not to drop value.
The stock doubled from its low. Wasn't the model right?
The outcome was good, but for reasons the model did not measure: repricing and policy. I judge the process, not the outcome.
Why Neutral now?
13x a 2026 floor that includes one-offs, shrinking membership and no 2027 guidance: upside and downside look balanced.
What one metric would you watch?
The Medicaid benefits ratio. Below about 92% the earnings recovery is real; stuck near 93.5% it is not.

Glossary

Health benefits ratio (HBR)
Medical costs divided by premium revenue; the core margin measure for an insurer.
Risk adjustment
Transfers between insurers based on members' health status, settled after the plan year.
Medicaid / Medicare
US public health programmes for low-income people (states) and for people over 65 (federal).
Guidance floor
A 'greater than' figure: the company commits to a minimum, not a midpoint.
