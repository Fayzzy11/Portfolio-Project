Why hold both

Strategy performance only: Horus hedged into euros, so the dollar plays no part. Monthly returns, January 2007 – June 2026, rebalanced to the target weights every month.

The two strategies are correlated at 0.67 overall and 0.46 in the months when world equities fall, because Horus keeps 40% in bonds while the value strategy holds equities and cuts them to 50% on its own signals. Once the dollar is taken out, they earn about the same (8.9% and 8.6% a year), so mixing them does not raise the return; it lowers the risk. The Sharpe ratio rises from 0.71 (value alone) and 0.68 (Horus alone) to about 0.77. The two answer different needs: a 60/40 for a balanced investor, a 100%-equity value strategy for an equity allocation.

What a euro investor who did not hedge actually got: Horus converted from dollars at the month-end EUR/USD rate. The dollar added about 1.3% a year to Horus over the period and rose in the 2008 crash, which cut its worst loss from −20% to −14%. That gain came from the currency, not from the strategy. Hedging: dollar return − dollar cash rate + euro cash rate each month.
