Multi-Asset Trend

Fourteen ETFs across equities, bonds and commodities. Each one is held only while its trend is up, sized by risk, with the rest in cash. Rebalanced monthly.

On this pageThe ideaRulesUniverseResultsFutures study

The idea

Markets that have risen over the past months tend to keep rising for a while, across asset classes and over a century of data (Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen, 2012; Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen, 2017). Trend following does not forecast; it follows, and it steps aside when a market turns down.

This version is built for a private investor in Europe: UCITS ETFs only, long or cash, monthly trades. It is the investable counterpart of my earlier futures study.

Rules

Fixed before the data extraction of 30 October 2026.

StepRule
Universe14 ETFs: 4 equity regions, 4 bond markets, 6 commodities.
SignalFor each asset, the sign of its excess return over cash on 3, 6 and 12 months, averaged: from −1 to +1. The same blend as the futures study, chosen there for robustness, not for its result.
PositionLong when the signal is positive, in proportion to it; cash otherwise. No short positions: an ETF investor cannot short cheaply.
SizingEach asset gets the same risk (inverse of its 12-month volatility); the portfolio is scaled to a 10% volatility target, with at most 100% invested.
RebalancingMonthly, at the first trading day of the month.
CashMoney-market rate (€STR, Euribor before 2019) on the uninvested part.
Costs0.10% per unit of turnover and 0.30% a year of ETF fees.
Test planJanuary 2001 to September 2026 in EUR; design period to 2015, validation afterwards, untouched. References: MSCI World Net EUR and a 60/40.

Futures study

The original study used 17 futures markets, long and short, from 2001 to 2026. Its 3/6/12-month blend earned 6.9% a year with a 0.64 Sharpe ratio; its long/cash ETF approximation 4.7% with 7.5% volatility. The full report stays online.
