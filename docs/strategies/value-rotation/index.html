Global Value Rotation

Each month, hold the four cheapest developed countries and the four cheapest sectors, measured against their own history. Eight UCITS ETFs, equal weight.

On this pageThe ideaRulesUniverseLink with the pitchesResultsRisks

The idea

Value works across markets, not only across stocks: countries and sectors that are cheap relative to their own history have tended to outperform over the following years (Asness, Moskowitz and Pedersen, 2013, Value and Momentum Everywhere). Countries and sectors are also easy to hold: one ETF each, cheap, liquid, available to any European investor.

The rotation is the top-down half of my value process. The stock-level screen and the pitches are the bottom-up half.

Measure

E/P, B/P and dividend yield of 25 indices at each month end.

Score

Each ratio versus the line's own 10-year history, averaged.

Select

The 4 cheapest countries and the 4 cheapest sectors.

Hold

Eight ETFs at 12.5% each until the next month end.

Rules

Fixed before any data was extracted. Changing a rule after seeing a result would be one more trial, and would weaken every statistic.

StepRule
Universe14 developed-country indices and the 11 GICS sectors of MSCI World: 25 lines, each held through one UCITS ETF.
SignalAverage of three z-scores: earnings yield (E/P), book yield (B/P) and dividend yield, each measured against the line's own trailing 10-year history (at least 5 years). A country is cheap when it is cheap versus its own past, so structurally cheap markets are not bought forever.
SelectionTwo sleeves of equal size: the 4 cheapest countries and the 4 cheapest sectors.
WeightingEqual weight, 12.5% per ETF; fully invested, long only.
RebalancingMonthly: signal at the last trading day of the month, trades on the first trading day of the next month.
Costs0.10% per unit of turnover and 0.25% a year of ETF fees.
BenchmarkMSCI World Net Total Return in EUR.
Test planBacktest January 2006 to September 2026 in EUR. Variants published whatever the result: 3 or 6 lines per sleeve, a 12-month momentum filter, countries only, sectors only.

The rules below were fixed on 30 October 2026, before the data were extracted. Monthly index data (2000-2026) are being extracted from the Bloomberg terminal; results will be published here with every variant tested.

Risks

Value traps at market level

A country can stay cheap for years (Italy, Hong Kong). Measuring cheapness against its own history limits this, but does not remove it.

Concentration

Eight lines can overlap: a cheap Financials sector and a cheap Italy are partly the same bet.

Growth markets

When expensive growth leads, as in 2023-24, a value rotation lags the benchmark.
