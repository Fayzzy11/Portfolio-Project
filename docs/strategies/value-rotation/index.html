Global Value Rotation

Value decides what to hold: the four cheapest developed countries and sectors against their own history. Trend decides when: in cash while world equities are below their 10-month average. Eight UCITS ETFs, monthly.

The idea

Value works across markets, not only across stocks: countries and sectors that are cheap relative to their own history have tended to outperform over the following years (Asness, Moskowitz and Pedersen, 2013, Value and Momentum Everywhere). Countries and sectors are also easy to hold: one ETF each, cheap, liquid, available to any European investor.

Value alone has a weakness: cheap markets are often cheap because they are falling, and they can keep falling with the rest of the market (2008). So the strategy splits the two questions. Value decides what to hold; trend decides when to hold it. The value signal is slow and is given time to work; the trend filter is fast and is checked every month.

The rotation is the top-down half of my value process. The stock-level screen and the pitches are the bottom-up half.

Measure

E/P, B/P and dividend yield of 25 indices at each month end.

Score

Each ratio versus the line's own 10-year history, averaged.

Select and keep

Buy the 4 cheapest countries and sectors; keep a line while it stays in the 6 cheapest.

Time

Invested while MSCI World is above its 10-month average, in cash otherwise.

Rules

Each version was written down before it was run; every version tried is published under Variants.

StepRule
Universe14 developed-country indices and the 11 GICS sectors of MSCI World: 25 lines, each held through one UCITS ETF.
Value signal (what)Average of three z-scores: earnings yield (E/P), book yield (B/P) and dividend yield, each measured against the line's own trailing 10-year history (at least 5 years). A market is cheap when it is cheap versus its own past.
SelectionTwo sleeves of equal size: the 4 cheapest countries and the 4 cheapest sectors.
Holding bufferA line already held is kept until it leaves the 6 cheapest of its sleeve. Value pays over years, not months: the buffer stops the strategy from selling a cheap market just because another became marginally cheaper.
Trend filter (when)At each month end, if MSCI World Net EUR is below its 10-month average, the whole portfolio moves to cash (euro money market) until it closes back above.
WeightingEqual weight, 12.5% per ETF when invested; long only, no leverage.
RebalancingMonthly: signals at the last trading day of the month, trades on the first trading day of the next month.
Costs0.10% per unit of turnover and 0.25% a year of ETF fees.
BenchmarkMSCI World Value Net EUR (style benchmark, chosen 4 Nov 2026 because the strategy is a value strategy); MSCI World Net EUR is also shown as the investor's alternative.
HistoryRules V1 fixed on 30 Oct 2026 before the data. V4c pre-registered on 4 Nov 2026 with three other candidates, then adopted. 2006-2015 is the design half, 2016-2026 the validation half; the live record starts in November 2026.

Year by year

What the backtest says. Over 2006-2026 the strategy earned 8.5% a year against 7.2% for MSCI World Value, with a worst loss of −19% against −54%. MSCI World, the investor's alternative, earned 9.0% with a −49% worst loss. The lead is concentrated: the strategy beat MSCI World Value in only 7 calendar years out of 21, and 2008 alone (4.7% against −37.7%) explains most of it; excluding 2008, it earned 8.5% a year against 10.0%. It led over 2006-2015 (9.2% against 4.7%) and lagged over 2016-2026 (7.8% against 9.5%). The trend filter kept the portfolio in cash 56 months out of 249, in 30 switches: it saved 2008 and 2022 and cost money in quick rebounds such as 2020. The value score itself has little predictive power at country and sector level: the strategy is best read as value exposure with crash protection, not as a source of steady excess return.

Risks

Value traps at market level

A country can stay cheap for years (Italy, Hong Kong). Measuring cheapness against its own history limits this, but does not remove it.

Concentration

Eight lines can overlap: a cheap Financials sector and a cheap Italy are partly the same bet.

False trend signals

The filter sells after a fall and buys back after a rebound. In sharp V-shaped recoveries (2020, 2023) it misses the rebound: 30 switches in 20 years, several of them costly.

Growth markets

When expensive growth leads, as in 2023-24, a value rotation lags the benchmark.
