Global Value Rotation

70% in ETFs on the four cheapest developed countries and four cheapest sectors against their own history; 30% in the eight cheapest stocks inside them. A risk rule decides how much to hold: 100%, or 50% when the market turns. Monthly.

The idea

Value works across markets and across stocks (Asness, Moskowitz and Pedersen, 2013, Value and Momentum Everywhere). The strategy uses both. Its core holds the countries and sectors that are cheap against their own history, through one ETF each. Its satellite holds the cheapest stocks inside them.

Why both? A sector ETF holds hundreds of companies: even when the sector is cheap, not all of them are. The satellite goes one level down and keeps only the names that are cheap within their sector — the signal is more precise there, and that is where most of the excess return came from. The ETF core keeps the portfolio diversified and cheap to run.

Value alone has a weakness: cheap markets are often cheap because they are falling, and they can keep falling with the rest of the market (2008). So the strategy splits the two questions. Value decides what to hold; a risk rule decides how much of it to hold — 100%, or 50% when the market turns, never zero, as a real fund would not either.

Rank markets

E/P, B/P and dividend yield of 25 country and sector indices against their own 10-year history.

Hold the core

8 ETFs: the 4 cheapest countries and 4 cheapest sectors, 70% of the portfolio.

Pick the stocks

8 stocks, the cheapest within their sector, inside the core's countries and sectors: 30%.

Dose the risk

100% invested, or 50% while at least two of three risk indicators are red.

Rules

Each rule was written down and committed before the test that used it was run.

StepRule
Structure70% ETF core (countries and sectors) + 30% stock satellite (single stocks). Both sleeves follow the same risk rule.
Core · universe14 developed-country indices and the 11 GICS sectors of MSCI World: 25 lines, each held through one UCITS ETF.
Core · value signalAverage of three z-scores: earnings yield (E/P), book yield (B/P) and dividend yield, each measured against the line's own trailing 10-year history (at least 5 years). A market is cheap when it is cheap versus its own past.
Core · selectionThe 4 cheapest countries and the 4 cheapest sectors, equal weight (8.75% each at full exposure). A line already held is kept until it leaves the 6 cheapest of its sleeve.
Satellite · universeThe 300 largest stocks of MSCI World at each half-year (point-in-time composition, delisted names included), one line per issuer.
Satellite · value signalComposite of EV/EBIT, book-to-price, free-cash-flow yield and sales-to-EV (book-to-price and earnings yield for financials), ranked within each sector. Accounts are used only six months after the fiscal year end and never before their publication.
Satellite · selection8 stocks, equal weight (3.75% each at full exposure), taken from the countries or sectors the core holds; at most 2 per sector and 3 per country. A stock is kept while it stays in the cheapest 40% of its sector.
Risk ruleThree indicators at each month end: trend (MSCI World Net EUR below its 10-month average), credit (euro high yield behind short euro government bonds over 3 months) and breadth (fewer than half of the 25 lines above their 10-month average). With at least two red, the whole strategy is cut to 50% equities and 50% euro money market, never to zero; back to 100% when fewer than two are red.
RebalancingMonthly: signals at the last trading day of the month, trades on the first trading day of the next month. Long only, no leverage.
CostsETFs: 0.10% per unit of turnover and 0.25% a year of fees. Stocks: 0.15% per unit of turnover and 0.40% a year for withholding tax on dividends.
BenchmarkMSCI World Value Net EUR (style benchmark); MSCI World Net EUR is also shown as the investor's alternative.
Periods2006-2015 is the design half, 2016-2026 the validation half. The live record starts in November 2026.

What the backtest says. From February 2006 to June 2026, the strategy earned 8.9% a year against 7.0% for MSCI World Value and 8.9% for MSCI World, with a worst loss of −31% against −54% and −49%. It led its benchmark in both halves (7.2% against 4.6% over 2006-2015, 10.7% against 9.3% over 2016-2026) and in 11 calendar years out of 21. Most of the excess return comes from the stock satellite (9.8% a year, t = 1.34): the value signal works within sectors, at stock level, much better than between countries and sectors. The ETF core mainly brings risk control: the risk rule held 50% in cash for 58 of 245 months, and the strategy lost 20.0% in 2008 against 37.7%. No gap is significant at the 5% level (t = 1.09); the versions were chosen after seeing the whole period, so the clean test is the live record from November 2026.

Risks

Value traps

A country can stay cheap for years (Italy, Hong Kong), and a stock can be cheap because its business is shrinking (tobacco). Ranking within sectors and against own history limits this, but does not remove it.

Stock-specific risk

Eight stocks at 3.75% each: one bad name moves the portfolio. The satellite alone earned 9.8% a year but lost −32% at worst; HBOS fell 60% in September 2008.

False signals

The risk rule cuts after a fall and adds back on a rebound. In 2008 the strategy lost 20% against 38% for its benchmark, but the rule costs money in short corrections that reverse quickly.

Growth markets

When expensive growth leads, as in 2023-24, a value strategy lags MSCI World even when it beats MSCI World Value.
